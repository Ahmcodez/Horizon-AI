Social Security's 2026 COLA: 2.8%, and what it actually buys you

The Social Security Administration confirmed a 2.8% cost-of-living adjustment for 2026 on October 24, 2025. Here's where that number comes from, what it adds to a typical check, and the part of the announcement most coverage leaves out.

Nearly 71 million Social Security beneficiaries and 7.5 million SSI recipients will see a 2.8% benefit increase, the SSA announced. For the average retired worker, that works out to roughly $56 more a month. SSI recipients see their increase first, in the payment dated December 31, 2025; everyone else sees it starting with the January 2026 payment, with COLA notices mailed in early December.

Where the 2.8% comes from

The COLA isn't a policy choice made each year — it's a formula. Social Security tracks the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) and compares the third quarter of the current year to the third quarter of the prior year. Whatever that index rose by, benefits rise by the same percentage the following January. For context, the 10-year average COLA has been 3.1%, and 2025's adjustment was 2.5%, so 2026 lands roughly in between.

The other number in the announcement: $184,500

The COLA isn't the only figure that moves. The maximum amount of earnings subject to Social Security payroll tax also rose for 2026, to $184,500, up from $176,100 in 2025. If you earn above that threshold, the extra income isn't taxed for Social Security — but it also doesn't add to your future benefit calculation.

Why your actual check might feel smaller than 2.8%

This is the part that catches people off guard every year. Most retirees have their Medicare Part B premium deducted automatically from their Social Security payment — and for 2026, that premium rose 9.7%, to $202.90 a month, well ahead of the 2.8% COLA. For someone near the standard premium tier, a meaningful chunk of the raise is absorbed before the deposit ever lands. It's not a Social Security cut; it's two separate adjustments landing in the same month, moving in the same direction but at very different speeds.

What to actually do with this

A COLA announcement is a good prompt to re-check your numbers, not just read the headline. The percentage is the same for everyone, but what it means for your household — after Medicare, after any IRMAA surcharge, against your own claiming-age plan — isn't. That's the calculation worth running with your real numbers rather than the national average.
