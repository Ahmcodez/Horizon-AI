Medicare Part B and IRMAA surcharges in 2026

Your Medicare premium comes straight out of your Social Security check before it ever reaches your bank account — and in 2026, it's taking a noticeably bigger bite. Here's exactly how much, and who pays more.

The standard Medicare Part B premium for 2026 is $202.90 a month, up from $185.00 in 2025 — a 9.7% jump, well ahead of this year's 2.8% Social Security cost-of-living adjustment. The annual Part B deductible rose too, from $257 to $283. For most beneficiaries, this premium is deducted automatically from their Social Security payment, so the increase shows up as a smaller net deposit even in a year when the gross benefit went up.

What IRMAA adds on top

If your income is above a set threshold, you pay more than the standard premium — a surcharge called the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount, or IRMAA. It's based on your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) from your tax return two years earlier, so your 2026 premium is set by your 2024 income.

Why this matters for your claiming decision

IRMAA catches people off guard most often right after retirement, when a final year of high W-2 income or a large one-time capital gain pushes their two-years-prior MAGI into a higher bracket — even though their actual current income has dropped. It also matters for Roth conversion planning: converting a large traditional IRA balance in one year can trigger IRMAA two years later, on top of the tax bill itself.

You can appeal it

If your income has genuinely dropped since the tax year being used — retirement, divorce, the death of a spouse, or another SSA-recognized life-changing event — you can file Form SSA-44 and ask Social Security to base your premium on your current income instead of your two-year-old tax return. This is one of the few Medicare-related numbers that's actually negotiable, but only if you ask.
